A Walkthrough for The Last Delivery

27 November 2026

Spoiler warning: this post walks through the order of events in The Last Delivery. Read on only if you want the route spelled out. I won't tell you how it ends, but I will tell you where to go.

The sun comes up over a town stopped mid-breath, and somewhere in your bag is a single newspaper whose headline is a warning. You have until noon. The subscriber list is torn, and the last address is missing. Here is the order that gets you from the depot to the town hall door without wandering in circles.

Start at the Paper Depot

Begin in the narrow plank shed where the last newspaper waits on the scarred wooden table. Take it. That's the delivery in your hands, and everything after this is about finding out where it goes. The depot smells of wet ink and cold dust, and the bare bulb overhead is the only light you'll get for free.

With the paper in your satchel, step out and get your bearings in the Town Square. The dry fountain, the leaning bandstand, the still awnings. Nothing moves. From here the town is small enough to walk, and the next stop is the one that smells of yeast.

Visit Bernadette at the Bakery

Head into the bakery. Bernadette is behind the counter with her hands on a fresh loaf, flour on her apron, and she'll call you sweetheart. She's warm but clipped, like someone who has been waiting too long for something.

Before you leave, get up to her apartment above the bakery. On the small writing desk is a folded note, its paper softened at the edges. Pick it up. It's a crumpled baker's note, and it belongs to Bernadette. Bring it back down and give it to her. That's the first real turn of the morning, and it opens the way forward.

Cross to the Post Office

Next, the cramped room of brass slots and a long wooden counter. The Postmaster stands behind the grille with a ledger he's already sorted twice, and he'll call you young man and nothing else. He's precise and dry, and he takes his time.

There's a heavy iron cellar door in the back wall, hinges dark, and it stays shut until you have the small brass key. Once you have it, use the brass key on that cellar door. That unlocks the Post Office Basement.

Down into the Basement

Take the steps down to the low stone cellar, damp walls and a dirt floor, lit by a single hanging bulb. A wooden crate has been prised open, and inside it lies a leather-bound ledger page, its edges curled and its ink faded but still legible. Pick it up.

Bring the ledger page back up and give it to the Postmaster. This is the step people miss, and it matters. He'll respond to it in his own dry way, and from that exchange you come away with the ornate town hall door key.

Up the lane to the Town Hall

Follow the Residential Lane north to its end, where a narrow footpath climbs toward the town hall. You pass the crooked houses with painted shutters and sagging porches, the bicycles against fences, the milk bottle on a stoop that no one has touched.

The broad flagstone landing at the top holds the town hall's main door, dark oak with a worn brass handle and an empty keyhole, beneath a clock stopped at ten past eleven. Use the ornate key you were given. The door opens onto the tall chamber of marble floors and dark wood paneling.

That's where the route ends for the purposes of this walkthrough. What happens in that chamber, and what the headline finally means, is yours to find out.

A note on pace

The town is small, and the whole route is really four moves: take the paper, deliver the note to Bernadette, open the basement and pass the ledger page to the Postmaster, then unlock the town hall. The trick is the middle, where the note and the ledger page look like set dressing and are not. If you're stuck, ask whether you're still carrying something someone else should have.
