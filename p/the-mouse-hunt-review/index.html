The Mouse Hunt: Three Cheeses and a Sleeping Cat

27 November 2026

Every so often a game shows up on Ascentery that fits the browser like a glove: small enough to finish in a sitting, big enough to keep you thinking. The Mouse Hunt is one of those. You are Pip, a mouse with an empty stomach and a nose that knows exactly what it wants. Three prizes are scattered through a quiet house, and none of them come easy.

A house with secrets in every room

The world is built from ordinary rooms made strange by scale. A living room with a worn rug and a dusty sofa is a landscape when you are mouse-sized. The bedroom holds a dressing table with a tarnished mirror and a hairpin half-hidden in the carpet. The music room has a piano under a sheet and a silver flute forgotten on a shelf. There is an attic sealed behind a warped door, a cellar of barrels and old potatoes, a pantry smelling of flour and dried herbs, a study with leather books and a mousetrap waiting under a cube of Swiss.

None of it is decoration. The hairpin, the flute, the sturdy stick by the fireplace, the squeaky rubber mouse in the attic dust: these are the tools of a very small heist.

What you are actually doing

The goal is simple to say and not simple to do. Gather Swiss, Cheddar and Brie, then bring them home to the Mouse Nest. Each cheese guards itself differently.

The Swiss sits on a mousetrap in the study, which asks for care rather than speed. The Brie is locked in a cupboard in the pantry, so you need a way in before you need a way out. And the Cheddar waits in the kitchen past a sleeping cat, curled by the stove with an ear that flicks toward any squeak.

That cat is the heart of the game. She does not speak in words, only a low rumbling purr when pleased and a sharp yowl when not. She will not chase what she cannot see. A squeaky rubber mouse left in the kitchen may buy you the Cheddar while she pounces. It is the kind of solution that makes you feel clever for spotting it, and slightly foolish for not spotting it sooner.

Why it works

The Mouse Hunt understands that a text adventure lives or dies on whether you can picture the room. Here the details do the work: dry still air in the attic, a ceiling low enough to make your fur prickle, warm light falling across the floorboards toward the mouse hole. The writing is spare and tactile, and the world is illustrated as you go, so the kitchen and its cat take shape in front of you.

As our host plays on video, the pleasure is in watching a plan come together piece by piece. Pip is not a hero. Pip is hungry, cautious and a little opportunistic, which is exactly right for a mouse. The tension comes from small things: a twitching tail, a creaking floorboard, a cupboard that will not open.

Who should play it

If you like puzzles that reward observation more than brute force, this is for you. If you enjoy stealth in miniature, where the stakes are one wedge of cheese and the threat is a cat who might wake up, this is for you. It suits a short evening session and anyone new to text adventures, since the goal is clear and the rooms are few but well used.
