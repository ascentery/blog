Errandale: A Walkthrough for the Unhurried

27 November 2026

Spoiler warning: this walkthrough lays out Errandale's order of operations. It does not reveal how the game ends, but it spoils the puzzle sequence. Stop here if you want to find Hollowmere cold.

Adventure games changed between the eighties and the nineties, and the change was not cosmetic. An 80s game was a wall. You threw verbs at it until something stuck. A 90s game was a conversation. It met you halfway, sometimes too far, sometimes with a shrug. Errandale sits on the 90s side of that line. Nothing here is designed to humiliate you. The challenge is remembering order.

Which is why a walkthrough is useful and also a small admission. The game will not punish you for wandering. It will simply let you wander for a long time.

The first errand

Begin in the Market. Among the trestles of turnips, apples and greens, a cabbage and an unlit torch lie on the ground. Take both. The torch matters later; the cabbage matters sooner. Near the corner, a covered stall sits shuttered under a tied-down cloth. Investigate it, and you will find a golden carrot. This is the first real object of the day, and it is not food.

Carry the carrot to the Bakery. The Baker works dough on the long table and calls you love or pet without meaning anything by it. Give her the carrot. In exchange she hands over a bag of flour, one of the supplies you came to Hollowmere for. Note the bucket of water by the counter; the room is arranged for work, not for you.

The town has other suppliers. At the Blacksmith, under the timber shelter, the smith hammers a red bar flat and speaks in short sentences. A silver hammer changes hands. At the General Store, narrow shelves of rope and lamp oil yield a leather waterskin. The Library, with its shelves out of true, gives up a dusty tome. Take the butter where you find it; the game is generous with small things.

The hooded figure

A hooded figure stands at the edge of Town Square, back against the bakery wall, hood low. He calls you adventurer. He is slow and measured, a man used to being listened to. Give him the dusty tome, and the sequence opens. This is the hinge of the game: without the book, the town stays politely closed.

He wants the cellar under the chapel cleared. That is the real errand. The flour and the hammer were always the smaller part.

The cellar

At the Guardhouse, pikes on whitewashed walls and a plank table of tally sheets, a rusty key waits on the table beside a golden carrot nobody has claimed. Take the key. The guardhouse chest stays locked and stays that way; do not spend time on it.

Take the path down to the Hidden Cellar beneath the chapel. The floor is strewn with torn sacks, gnawed bones, and a bucket on its side. The key opens the way. Inside, giant rats have made a home in the far corner's mound of straw and rags. Clear them. This is not a puzzle; it is a chore with teeth. Bring the torch, and bring patience.

The clerk's path to the gate

With the rats gone, gather what the town asked for: the bag of flour, the silver hammer, the book, the greens. Then return to the Town Gate and tell the captain you are finished. The gate stands open in the low stone wall, flanked by watch posts and a bell rope. The porter will talk your ear off. Let him.

Two omissions will cost you. First, the Edge of the Woods is worth visiting before you leave; Wren keeps an arrow counted and re-counted, Kael reads the ground, and neither will tell you what you missed. Second, the Creek Crossing and the Woods Above the Town are on the way. The path is a deer trail; the crossing stones sit at ankle depth. Walk it once so you know what the town looks like from outside the wall.

Why the order holds

The carrot before the baker, the baker before the flour, the tome before the cellar, the key before the rats, the rats before the gate. It is a chain of dependencies, which is the 90s compromise: the game is not hiding the door, it is asking you to knock on the right one first. An eighties designer would have made the carrot a riddle. Errandale makes it a cabbage's neighbour.

The two elves help. Wren is dry and quick and calls you boss. Kael speaks little and calls you by name. They follow you through all of it, which is a kindness the earlier decade rarely offered.
